    Sources, method & caveats

    Air quality. Station annual means for NO₂ and PM2.5 from the European Environment Agency (AirQualityStatistics, validated E1a data, data-coverage filter applied), 2013–2025, for EU members, the Western Balkans and Kosovo. Ukraine and Belarus are city-level means from the WHO Ambient Air Quality Database v8 (June 2026), which has no traffic/background split. Moldova reports no data to either source.

    Cities. Urban and suburban stations are grouped to the largest GeoNames city whose catchment covers them (radius grows with population, max 20 km). Population is the GeoNames city figure, not the metro area.

    Measures. Traffic stations sit beside busy roads; background stations measure the general urban air most residents breathe at home. Roadside excess = traffic minus background, a direct read of the local vehicle contribution. NO₂ is the main traffic marker; road transport is roughly a third to a half of national NOx emissions and a much larger share at street level. Population exposure KPIs use background stations where a city has them, otherwise the all-station average.

    Thresholds (annual mean, µg/m³). WHO 2021 guideline: NO₂ 10, PM2.5 5. EU limit from 2030 (Directive 2024/2881): NO₂ 20, PM2.5 10. Current EU limit: NO₂ 40, PM2.5 25.

    Vehicle fleet. Eurostat road_eqs_carhab, road_eqs_carage, road_eqs_carpda; NOx road-transport share (exhaust, NFR 1A3b i–iv) from env_air_emis.

    Caveats. Station networks differ by country and change over time, so year-to-year changes for one city can reflect stations opening or closing. Coverage is a sample of cities, not the whole population. Values are measurements, not modelled exposure.
